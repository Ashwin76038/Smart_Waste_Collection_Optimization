# Operational recommendations from the simulated pilots

These are decisions to **test**, not claims about observed Chennai or Coimbatore service. The route comparisons hold each city’s pilot demand, required stops and truck constraints fixed between its baseline and optimized order. The municipalities have not supplied bin sensor histories, route GPS, fleet details or verified depots. Coimbatore’s 0.9 demand multiplier is a declared scenario assumption.

| Action | Evidence in this project | Decision boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Prioritize a field audit in Chennai wards `108` and `129`, and Coimbatore wards `055` and `052`. | Within-city synthetic overflow is highest there: Chennai 123.72 and 108.85 L/bin-day; Coimbatore 94.31 and 89.93 L/bin-day. The [ward audit table](phase5_model/ward_audit_candidates.csv) includes required bins and boundary source. | Verify real bin inventory, fill, pedestrian catchment, local ward boundary and collection constraints before changing frequency or adding bins. Cross-city ward rankings are invalid. |
| Trial a three-truck, road-network route order for the pilot. | At the 80% trigger, matched simulated route totals fall **57.34→45.50 km (20.66%)** in Chennai and **38.46→27.97 km (27.26%)** in Coimbatore. Both serve every required pilot bin. | Confirm depot/receiving entrances, truck access, real payloads and shifts. These are one-date planning scenarios; no recurring or realized savings estimate follows. |
| Keep a capacity check before reducing the pilot fleet. | Full-bin reserve is 25,160 L in Chennai and 25,180 L in Coimbatore, above two 12,000 L trucks. One/two-truck base alternatives are infeasible under this conservative rule. | This proves only reserve-capacity infeasibility for the stated pilot, not that two actual trucks could never serve measured loads or make multiple trips. |
| Treat 70/80/90% thresholds as service tradeoffs, not a universal policy. | The [threshold table](phase5_model/threshold_tradeoffs.csv) shows 70% raises required stops from 36→46 Chennai and 34→42 Coimbatore. Chennai’s 70% optimized order **increases** isolated 24 h spill versus its matched baseline by 7.35 L, while Coimbatore’s 70% order avoids 43.11 L. A 90% trigger reduces stops but changes service and spill exposure. | Select a threshold only after field-calibrated demand and a longer multi-day policy replay. Route distance alone is not a service objective; include spill/overdue penalties in future optimization. |
| Use predicted fill to create a review queue, with staff oversight for near-full risks and access exceptions. | Separate city ridge models beat the simple moving-average baseline on synthetic held-out test data (Chennai MAE 8.17 vs 19.65; Coimbatore 7.69 vs 20.57 fill points). Near-full recall is only **56.18%/57.97%**, and nominal 90% intervals cover less than 90%. | Do not automatically dispatch from a claimed calibrated overflow probability. Preserve telemetry age, recent collection, current fill and access rules in the queue. Refit using real policy feedback before operational use. |
| Verify local economics before procurement or a savings target. | Simulated base road savings imply 2.63 L/INR 243.20 Chennai and 2.33 L/INR 215.22 Coimbatore under **assumed 4.5 km/L** and the same historical Chennai diesel price. | Coimbatore lacks a sourced local fuel/procurement price. Wages, idling, lift/PTO use and maintenance are excluded. Neither value is a budget saving. |

Do not recommend moving or purchasing bins from the synthetic hotspot map alone. The geographic locations use real OSM nodes, but placement, demand and overflows are hypothetical. A relocation case needs actual overflow complaints, repeated measured fill, walkshed coverage, waste type and placement constraints. The existing [comparison report](../14_outputs/reports/city_expansion/comparison_report.md) and [Phase 4 methodology](../docs/phase4_methodology.md) explain the simulator and one-day counterfactual.
